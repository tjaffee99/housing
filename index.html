    About this map

    Every occupied home in the United States and Puerto Rico is on this map: about 85 million owner-occupied homes and 45 million rented ones. Zoomed in, each dot is 10 homes; zoomed out, each dot stands for more (the panel shows how many). All prices are in current dollars, with no upper limit.

    Hover or tap anywhere for the area's numbers; click for details. From the keyboard, focus the map, move with the arrow keys and press Enter to open the area under the crosshair.

    Three ways to look at prices

    • Home values: owner-occupied homes, by what they are worth today.
    • Rents: rented homes, either by market rent, what the home would rent for if it were listed today, or by what tenants pay, which includes long-held and rent-regulated leases (in much of New York or San Francisco the two differ by thousands of dollars a month). Rents include utilities.
    • Monthly cost: every home on one scale. Owner-occupied homes show what buying that home today would cost a month (20% down, a 30-year fixed mortgage at today's average rate, property tax and insurance); rented homes show their market rent. It sets places where most people rent (Lower Manhattan) beside places where nearly everyone owns (Atherton). Buying and Renting show each group on its own.

    Size switches to ZIP codes colored by the typical price of a home with that many bedrooms: Zillow's home values by bedroom count, HUD's Fair Market Rents (what a recent mover pays at the 40th percentile), and the Census median rent paid.

    Where the numbers come from

    The base is the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, 2020–2024: each of the country's 242,000 block groups' distribution of home values (owners' estimates) and gross rents, in brackets. A block group's dots are drawn bracket by bracket from its own distribution, so a dot's color is a price for the homes it stands for, not a neighborhood average.

    • Today's prices. Values are moved from the survey years to today with the Zillow Home Value Index for the home's ZIP code (its top-tier index for expensive homes); rents tenants pay, with the regional CPI for rent.
    • No top code. The Census stops at "$2 million or more" and "$3,500 or more". Homes above $2M get prices from 2024–2025 mortgage records (HMDA) for purchases of $2M+ homes in the same tract, blended with similar tracts where sales are few; where most homes are over $2M, the ZIP code's typical value is matched to Zillow's. Rents above $3,500 follow a Pareto tail fitted to each tract's upper brackets.
    • Market rents. In each tract, households who moved in since 2023 pay more than the typical renter (Census table B25113); the cheapest, longest-held rentals are raised the most and the priciest not at all. Where Zillow observes asking rents for a ZIP code (ZORI), market rents are raised to at least that level.

    Where the dots go

    Dots are placed at random inside the block group's 2020 census blocks, in proportion to how many housing units each block had, so they land where homes are and not in parks, lakes or empty land. A dot's exact position is not the location of any real home.

    Things to know

    • Home values are owners' own estimates, adjusted to today; they are not sale prices.
    • Block-group estimates come from small samples and have wide margins of error. Tract and county figures are more reliable, and prices far above $2M or $3,500 are modeled, not measured.
    • Vacant homes (for sale, for rent, seasonal) are not shown. Renter homes with no cash rent are gray under What tenants pay.
    • The 2024 release has no figures for 39 block groups in Suffolk County, NY (most of Brentwood and Wyandanch). Their homes are placed using the county total minus the published block groups.
